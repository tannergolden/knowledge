# 🕊️ Library / Religion & Spirituality /

## Purpose

Religion and spirituality studied neutrally, with every tradition treated as a peer and none privileged. Each tradition has its own division, whatever the aspect studied; comparative study, mythology, theology, contemplative practice and ritual have theirs for what spans traditions or treats religion in general.

## Divisions

| Division (Layer 3) | Scope |
|--------------------|-------|
| Comparative Religion | Cross-tradition comparison, methods of religious studies, interfaith analysis |
| Abrahamic Traditions | Judaism, Christianity, Islam, and related movements and scriptures |
| Dharmic Traditions | Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Sikhism, and related South Asian paths |
| East Asian Traditions | Taoism, Confucianism, Shinto, and folk and syncretic East Asian religion |
| Indigenous & Folk Traditions | Native, tribal, animist, and local folk religious systems worldwide |
| Mythology & Folklore | Sacred myths, cosmologies, and legends as belief |
| Theology & Doctrine | Systematic theology, creeds, scriptural interpretation, doctrine |
| Contemplative & Secular Spirituality | Meditation, mysticism, and nonreligious spiritual practice |
| Ritual & Practice | Rites, liturgy, festivals, pilgrimage, prayer, and observance |

## Class here / not here

- Class here: Any tradition studied neutrally, as a peer, in its own division, whatever the aspect
- Class here: Theology, myth, ritual, and contemplative practice in general or across traditions, in the division for that aspect
- Class here: Sacred myth and cosmology as belief
- Not here: Abstract ethics or metaphysics argued without a tradition - goes to Philosophy & Mind
- Not here: The sociology, demography, or politics of religious groups - goes to Society & Social Sciences
- Not here: The history of a faith or of a sacred site - goes to History & Geography
- Not here: Folktales and proverbs as verbal art - goes to Language & Literature / Oral & Folk Literature
