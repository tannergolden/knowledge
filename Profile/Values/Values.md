# 🧭 Values

What the user prizes and how they judge right from wrong - the facet the AI consults when **helping weigh options or give advice**. Grounded in Schwartz's theory of basic human values, Moral Foundations, and Self-Determination Theory. Substantial standalone beliefs (religious, political, philosophical) live as their own `sensitive` entries so a benign query never surfaces them.

## Core Values

The user's ranked core values (cap at ~5 - forcing the ranking is the point). Schwartz groups the ten basic values into four higher-order families; use them to check coverage:

- **Openness to change** (self-direction, stimulation):
- **Self-enhancement** (achievement, power):
- **Conservation** (security, conformity, tradition):
- **Self-transcendence** (benevolence, universalism):

## Orientations

Where the user sits on high-leverage axes that shape how the AI should frame advice:

- **Change vs. stability:**
- **Ambition:** (wired to build, to maintain, or between)
- **Money:** (tool, goal, neutral, source of stress)
- **Health:** (optimize, maintain, react when broken)
- **Risk:** (seek, tolerate, avoid)
- **Self vs. others:** (individual achievement vs collective good)
- **Tradition vs. progress:**
- **Short-term vs. long-term:** (default time horizon)

## Life Philosophy

How the user views life, what they believe about people and work, and any motto or idea they live by.

## Ethical Orientation

How the user reasons about right and wrong - their ethical framework (rule-based, outcomes-based, virtue) and the Moral Foundations (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty) they weight most. Plus principles lived by, lines they will not cross, and non-negotiables.

## Motivation

What drives the user (Self-Determination Theory: autonomy, competence, relatedness; intrinsic vs extrinsic). What energizes them and what drains them.

## Purpose & Meaning

What gives the user's life meaning, what they feel called to do, and the legacy they want to leave.

## Definition of Success

What a good life or good work looks like to the user, in their own terms.

## Role Models & Influences

People the user admires and why, plus books, ideas, or movements that shaped them - an indirect, high-signal read on aspired-to values.

## Anti-Role-Models

People or patterns the user actively does not want to become - what they define themselves against.

## What I Stand For

The commitments the user actively champions.

## What I Stand Against

The things the user actively opposes.
